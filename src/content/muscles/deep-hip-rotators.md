Six small muscles running from the pelvis to the top of the thigh bone, doing at the hip roughly
what the rotator cuff does at the shoulder: holding the ball steady in the socket while the big
muscles move it.

Piriformis is the one people have heard of, usually because the sciatic nerve passes beneath it
and sometimes through it. Tightness there can produce buttock pain that travels down the leg —
which is worth knowing about, and is also a description that fits several other things, so it is
not a self-diagnosis.

For a knee reference these matter through rotation. The kneecap runs in a groove cut into the
thigh bone; if the thigh bone is poorly controlled in rotation, that groove moves beneath the
kneecap. It is the same mechanism as gluteus medius, one layer deeper.
