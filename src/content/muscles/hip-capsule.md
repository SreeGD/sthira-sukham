The hip capsule is not the knee capsule with a different address, and the difference is
worth knowing.

Its ligaments run in a **spiral**. As the hip straightens, they wind tighter and pull the
ball firmly into the socket; as it bends, they unwind and slacken. This is why a stiff hip
so often feels worst when you try to stand up tall and stretch the leg behind you, and why
it can feel deceptively free when you are sitting with the hip already bent.

The front of the capsule carries the **iliofemoral ligament**, the strongest ligament in
the body. It is the reason a hip can bear standing all day without muscular effort — you
hang on it. It is also the reason a hip that has spent years in a chair, never fully
straightened, loses extension quietly and without pain until something demands it.

Like all capsular tissue, it responds to being moved rather than to being strengthened. It
cannot be switched on. It can only be visited regularly at the end of its range.

Capsular restriction at the hip has a distinguishing feel: firm, springy, arriving at the
same point every time, and not much improved by warming up. Muscular restriction eases as
you move. Capsular restriction largely does not.
