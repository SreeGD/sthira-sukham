The muscle you can feel tighten on the front of your shin when you lift your foot toward you.

It is the opposite number to the calf, and the imbalance between them is usually lopsided: the
calf shortens with disuse and footwear, while tibialis anterior weakens. That combination is why
ankle problems more often present as too little upward movement than as too little downward.

Its relevance here is mostly about control rather than range — it is what stops the foot slapping
down and what lifts the toes clear of the ground on each step.
