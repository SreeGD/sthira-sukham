Small, deep, and responsible for something the knee could not do without it.

A fully straightened knee is not simply at the end of its range — it is mechanically locked.
The joint surfaces rotate slightly into a close-packed position that lets you stand without
holding yourself up with muscle. It is efficient, and it has to be undone before the knee
can bend again.

Popliteus is what undoes it, rotating the shin inward a few degrees to release the lock.
This is why it earns a place in a stiffness reference despite its size: if you notice
resistance in the very first moment of bending from fully straight, rather than at the far
end of the bend, this muscle is a plausible part of the story.
