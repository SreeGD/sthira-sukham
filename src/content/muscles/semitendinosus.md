Named for its unusually long tendon, which you can feel as a distinct cord on the inner side
of the back of the knee. That tendon is frequently harvested as a graft in ligament
reconstruction, so anyone who has had that surgery has a specific reason to pay attention
to this muscle.

With semimembranosus it forms the medial hamstring group, rotating the lower leg inward —
the opposite of what biceps femoris does laterally. The knee's rotational position at rest
reflects the balance between them.
