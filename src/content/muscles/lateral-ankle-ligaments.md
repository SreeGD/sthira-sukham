These are the ligaments people tear when they "go over" on an ankle. They are on the outer
side, and they resist the foot rolling inward.

The reason they belong in a reference about stiffness is a pairing that sounds contradictory
until you have felt it: **after a sprain, an ankle is often stiff and unstable at the same
time.** Those seem like opposites. They are not.

The tissue heals, but it heals disorganised and thickened — so the joint loses range. At the
same time the nerve endings inside the ligament, which report where the foot is in space,
are damaged and report less well — so the ankle is slower to react when the ground is not
where it expected. Restricted, and untrustworthy, from the same event.

This matters for what helps. Stretching alone addresses half of it. The half that is about
the ankle knowing where it is responds to balance work — standing on one leg, on progressively
less predictable surfaces — which trains reporting rather than length.

An ankle that gives way without warning is a red flag, not a training target. Have it looked
at before working on it.
