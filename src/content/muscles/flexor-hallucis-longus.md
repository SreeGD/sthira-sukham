This muscle explains a specific and often misread complaint: **pain or restriction at the
inner ankle that is actually coming from the big toe, or the reverse.**

It has an unusually long run. It begins deep in the back of the shin, its tendon hooks around
behind the inner ankle bone, then travels the whole length of the sole to the tip of the big
toe. Anything that shortens it can announce itself anywhere along that path.

For the foot, its importance is directional and easy to get backwards. The muscle *bends the
toe down*. So when it is tight, what you lose is the toe **bending up** — and bending up is
the motion walking requires on every step, and the motion the windlass mechanism depends on
to stiffen the arch.

The everyday test is kneeling with the toes tucked under. If that position is limited at the
big toe rather than at the ankle or the knee, this is a likely contributor. It is a common
sticking point in seated postures for exactly that reason.

It is also the muscle that fires when you nearly lose your balance and the big toe grips the
floor. That reflex is a reasonable indication that it is still working.
