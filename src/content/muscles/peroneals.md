These are the ankle's brakes against rolling over, and they are the structure most consistently
left impaired after a sprain.

What tends to be lost is not raw strength so much as speed — the muscle responds a fraction late,
which is enough for the ankle to have gone over before the correction arrives. That is the
mechanism usually offered for why one sprain makes the next more likely.

It is also why balance work features in ankle rehabilitation more prominently than stretching.
The problem is a timing problem, and timing is trained by asking for corrections, not by
lengthening tissue.
