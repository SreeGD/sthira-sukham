These are the tendons that stand up on the top of your foot when you lift your toes. They are
easy to see, which makes them one of the few structures here you can check on yourself
without any equipment.

Their interest is less about their own tightness and more about what their behaviour reveals.
They are **assistants**. Their main job is lifting the toes; helping lift the whole foot is a
secondary role. When the primary foot-lifter at the front of the shin is weak, these take
over — and because they were not built for it, they become prominent, cordy and quick to
fatigue.

So a foot that shows hard, standing tendons whenever it lifts, and toes that claw upward
during balance work, is usually telling you the shin muscle behind them is not pulling its
weight. The answer in that case is not to stretch the toes. It is to strengthen the front of
the shin.

At the other end, tightness here restricts the toes curling *down*, which is what makes
sitting back on the heels with the feet flat uncomfortable across the top of the foot for
some people — a different limitation from the far more common one of the toes not bending
upward far enough.
