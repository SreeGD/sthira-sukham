This is a small joint with an outsized say in how you walk.

Every step ends with the heel lifting and the body rolling forward over the big toe, which
means bending the toe upward by roughly 65°. No other joint in this reference is asked for
so much of its range so many times a day.

When the capsule and the joint surfaces stiffen, that range goes, and the body finds it
elsewhere. The usual substitution is to roll off the *inside* of the foot rather than over
the toe — which turns the whole foot inward, and hands the consequences up the chain to the
knee.

The end feel here is worth distinguishing. Muscular restriction is springy and eases as you
move. Capsular restriction is firm and consistent. Restriction from arthritic change at this
joint tends to be **hard and abrupt** — bone approaching bone — and it often comes with a
visible bump on the top of the joint.

That bump is the reason to act early rather than wait. The orthopaedic guidance is explicit
that this is easier to treat when caught before bone spurs have developed, and by the time
the bump is obvious they generally have.
