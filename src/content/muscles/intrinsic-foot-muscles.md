These are the muscles most people never think about, largely because you cannot see them
work and they have no impressive name.

They start and end inside the foot. They are what holds the arch up **actively** — the
counterpart to the plantar fascia, which holds it up passively. Two systems for one job, and
that redundancy matters: when the muscular half stops contributing, the passive half carries
the whole load. Passive tissue does not adapt to being overloaded the way muscle does. It
just gets irritated.

The usual finding is weakness, not tightness, which makes this one of the few structures in
this reference where stretching is beside the point.

**A stiff, supportive shoe does this job for the foot**, and a foot whose job is being done
does less of it. This is not an argument against supportive footwear — it is an argument for
spending some time each day asking the foot to hold itself up. Standing barefoot, and
deliberate work like short-foot holds, are how you make the request.

The clearest sign they are not contributing: the toes curl and grip when you try to balance.
That is the long muscles from the lower leg substituting for the small ones in the foot.
