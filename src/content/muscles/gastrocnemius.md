Gastrocnemius belongs in a knee reference for one reason: its two heads originate on the
femur, *above* the knee joint. It is an ankle muscle that crosses the knee.

That gives it the same position-dependence as rectus femoris and the hamstrings. Straighten
the knee and gastrocnemius is on stretch; bend the knee and it goes slack. This is exactly
why a calf stretch feels completely different with a straight versus bent knee — the
straight-knee version is stretching this muscle, the bent-knee version has taken it out of
play and is reaching the soleus underneath.
