The lateral hamstring, and the one people most often notice, because its tendon stands out
prominently on the outer side of the back of the knee.

Its long head crosses the hip as well as the knee, so like rectus femoris at the front it
responds to what both joints are doing. This is why hamstring length is tested with the hip
flexed — with the hip straight, the muscle is slack and the test tells you little.

Its outward rotation of the lower leg also makes it a factor in how the knee sits at rest,
not only in how far it bends.
