The ankle capsule is thin at the front and back, where the joint has to travel, and
thickened at the sides into ligaments that stop the foot rolling over. That arrangement is
why the ankle is stable side to side and mobile front to back, and why stiffness here is
almost always about dorsiflexion.

**There is one test worth knowing, and it costs nothing.** Kneel or stand facing a wall,
foot flat, and drive the knee forward over the toes. Then do it again with the knee locked
straight. A calf that is short will let the knee travel noticeably further when it is bent,
because bending the knee takes gastrocnemius out of the equation. A capsule that has
tightened will stop the knee at the same point either way.

This is worth the two minutes because the two problems have entirely different answers.
Calf tightness responds to stretching. Capsular tightness responds to the joint being taken
to the end of its range regularly — repeated movement rather than a long hold.

Capsular tightening is common after any period in a boot or a cast, where the joint is held
still for weeks. It is not caused by the injury so much as by the stillness.
