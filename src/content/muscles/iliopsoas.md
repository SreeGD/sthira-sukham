  No direct action on the knee. It is the main hip flexor, and it determines whether the hip

    The hip cannot straighten fully, so you stand and walk with it slightly bent. The pelvis

  A hip that cannot fully straighten leaves you standing with it slightly flexed, and the

  that all day and the knee never visits full extension. The quadriceps works continuously to
  hold you up, and the knee stiffens in a range it has stopped leaving. The restriction lives
  at the hip; the symptom appears at the knee.

The primary casualty of sitting, and the clearest illustration of why a stiff knee is often a
problem somewhere else.

Sitting holds the hip flexed for hours at a time. Iliopsoas adapts to that as its resting
length, and full hip extension becomes unavailable — not through damage, but through disuse.

The knock-on is what matters here. Standing with a hip that will not fully straighten means
standing with a slightly bent knee, because that is what keeps you balanced over the foot.
The knee then spends its entire day in the middle of its range, never reaching either end,
with the quadriceps working continuously to stop it buckling. The knee feels tight and tired.
Work on the knee and little changes, because the knee is not where the problem started.
