If one muscle explains why a knee reference spends this much time on the hip, it is this one.

Gluteus medius holds the pelvis level when you are on one leg — which, during walking, is
most of the time. When it is not working, the pelvis drops and the thigh bone rotates
inward on every step.

The knee is attached to the top of that rotation. The kneecap groove is cut into the femur,
so if the femur rotates, the groove rotates with it while the kneecap is held in place by
the quadriceps above. The relationship between kneecap and groove changes without the
kneecap having moved at all. This is the mechanism behind a great deal of front-of-knee
stiffness, and it is the reason so much of the strength work in this library targets the hip.
