The VMO gets more attention than any other muscle around the knee, and the reason is its
lowest fibres. They run nearly sideways, so they pull the kneecap medially — the only
meaningful counterweight to everything pulling it laterally.

It is also unusually easy to switch off. Fluid in the joint reduces its activation, and
because effusion often accompanies exactly the problems that need this muscle working, the
result can be self-sustaining: the knee swells, the VMO stops firing, the kneecap tracks
less well, and the joint stays irritated.

Whether the VMO can be trained in isolation from the rest of the quadriceps is genuinely
debated. What is not debated is that the whole quadriceps group tends to be inhibited after
knee swelling and benefits from being deliberately reactivated.
