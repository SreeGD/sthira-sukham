The labrum is a ring of cartilage around the rim of the hip socket. It makes the socket
deeper than the bone alone would be, and it seals the joint fluid inside, which is what lets
the hip glide with almost no friction.

It matters here for one reason: **it produces a different kind of restriction, and confusing
it with a tight muscle leads people to stretch harder into exactly the thing that hurts.**

Capsular and muscular restriction both feel like a range that gradually runs out. Labral and
impingement-type restriction feels like the hip moving freely and then hitting something — a
pinch, a catch, or a click, felt deep in the front of the groin, typically at the bottom of a
deep squat or when sitting low with the knees high.

That distinction is why this page describes the structure but recommends nothing for it. A
pinch at the end of hip bending is a sign to get the hip looked at, not a sign to stretch
into it. Deep hip stretching is the standard response, and it is the wrong one here.

The international consensus statement on hip impingement is explicit that a diagnosis needs
symptoms, clinical signs *and* imaging together — which is precisely why a reference like
this one cannot tell you whether you have it.
