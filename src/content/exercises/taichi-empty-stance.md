---
name: Empty stance
traditionalName: Xu Bu (empty step)
tradition: Tai chi (a fundamental stance across styles)
modality: taichi-qigong
startPosition: standing
targets: [vastus-medialis, vastus-lateralis, gluteus-medius, soleus]
goal: [strength, motor-control]
instructions:
  - Stand with feet about shoulder-width apart, then step one foot a short way forward.
  - Shift almost all your weight onto the back leg, bending that knee, and rest only the toe or ball of the front foot lightly on the floor.
  - Keep your torso upright over the back hip rather than leaning back.
  - Hold, then change sides. The front foot should be able to lift without you shifting.
dosage: Commonly held for 20 to 60 seconds per side.
difficulty: intermediate
regressions: [taichi-weight-shifting]
progressions: [taichi-heel-toe-walking]
contraindications:
  - Balance problems without support within reach
  - Knee pain on the loaded side
  - Recent knee surgery without clearance
stopIf:
  - Pain in the loaded knee
  - The loaded knee drifts inward
  - Trembling you cannot control
equipment: [none, chair]
evidenceLabel: moderate-evidence
sources: [wang-taichi-knee-oa-2016, neumann-kinesiology-2016]
---

Almost all the weight on one bent leg, held still. It is the quietest-looking hard thing in
this library.

The test built into the stance is elegant: if you can lift the front foot without shifting
your body first, the weight really was where you thought it was. Most people discover they
were leaving a third of it forward.

Holding a bent knee under load without moving asks the quadriceps to work continuously
rather than in bursts, and asks the hip to keep the pelvis level the whole time. It is the
same demand as standing on one leg, with a light front foot available as a safety net —
which makes it usable by people for whom true single-leg stance is not yet.
