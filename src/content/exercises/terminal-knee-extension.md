---
name: Terminal knee extension
modality: clinical-rom
startPosition: supine
targets: [vastus-medialis, vastus-lateralis, vastus-intermedius]
goal: [strength, motor-control]
quickSteps:
  - Put a rolled towel under the knee.
  - Lift only the heel to straighten the knee.
  - Squeeze the thigh. Hold 5 seconds.
  - Lower the heel slowly.
keyPoint: >-
  The back of the knee stays pressed into the roll throughout.
feelItIn: >-
  The muscle just above and inside the kneecap tightening.
commonMistake: >-
  Lifting the whole leg instead of just the heel.
instructions:
  - Sit or lie with a rolled towel or small cushion under the knee so it rests slightly bent.
  - Straighten the knee by lifting only the heel, keeping the back of the knee pressed down onto the roll.
  - Hold the fully straight position for a few seconds, feeling the muscle just above and inside the kneecap tighten.
  - Lower the heel slowly back down.
dosage: Commonly described as 10 to 15 repetitions with 5-second holds, two or three times a day.
difficulty: beginner
progressions: [pilates-standing-leg-pump]
regressions: [patellar-mobilisation]
contraindications:
  - Recent knee surgery where your surgeon has restricted active straightening
  - Sharp pain at the front of the knee on straightening
stopIf:
  - Pain at the front of the knee during the lift
  - You cannot achieve the last part of straightening at all — that is worth having looked at
  - Marked grinding or clunking
equipment: [towel, cushion]
evidenceLabel: mechanistic-rationale
sources: [neumann-kinesiology-2016, aaos-knee-arthritis]
---

The last fifteen degrees of knee straightening are disproportionately important and
disproportionately hard to recover. They are where the quadriceps has the least mechanical
advantage, and they are the first thing lost when the muscle is inhibited by swelling.

This exercise isolates precisely that segment. The roll under the knee means you start
already partly straightened, so all the work happens in the range that actually needs it,
rather than being spent in the easy middle of the movement.

If you cannot complete the last part of the straightening at all — the heel lifts but the
knee stays bent — that gap has a name and it is worth showing to a physiotherapist rather
than working around.
