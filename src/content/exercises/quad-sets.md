name: Quadriceps sets
modality: clinical-rom
startPosition: supine
targets:
  - vastus-medialis
  - vastus-lateralis
  - vastus-intermedius
  - rectus-femoris
goal:
  - strength
  - motor-control
quickSteps:
  - Lie down, towel under your ankle.
  - Tighten the thigh, press the knee down.
  - Hold 5 to 10 seconds. Keep breathing.
  - Release fully before the next one.
keyPoint: Feel the muscle actually tighten. If nothing happens, nothing is being trained.
feelItIn: The whole front of the thigh firming up.
commonMistake: Holding your breath. Breathe normally throughout.
instructions:
  - label: Get into position
    detail: Lie on your back with the leg straight out in front of you and a small rolled towel
      under your ankle, so the knee is unsupported.
  - label: Tighten the thigh
    detail: Tighten the muscle on the front of the thigh, pressing the back of the knee gently down
      toward the floor.
  - label: Hold and breathe
    detail: Hold the contraction, keeping breathing normal rather than holding your breath.
  - label: Release fully
    detail: Release slowly and completely before the next repetition.
dosage:
  reps: 10 to 20 repetitions
  sets: 1 to 3 sets per session
  frequency: 2 to 3 times a day
  hold: 5 seconds at the end of range
  note: Commonly described as 10 repetitions with holds of 5 to 10 seconds, several times a day.
difficulty: beginner
progressions:
  - straight-leg-raise
  - terminal-knee-extension
contraindications:
  - Recent knee surgery where your surgeon has restricted quadriceps activation
  - Sharp pain at the front of the knee on tightening
stopIf:
  - Front-of-knee pain during the contraction
  - Cramping in the thigh that does not settle
  - You cannot feel the muscle tighten at all — worth showing to a physiotherapist rather than
    pushing on
equipment:
  - towel

  - neumann-kinesiology-2016

The smallest useful thing you can ask a knee to do, and often the first.

Its value is entirely about inhibition. Swelling reduces the quadriceps' ability to switch
on, and the muscle does not reliably switch back on when the swelling settles — so what
looks like weakness is frequently a recruitment problem. A quad set asks for activation
with no movement at all, which is exactly what a joint that will not tolerate movement can
still give.

The towel under the ankle matters. Without it the knee rests on the floor and there is
nothing to press into; with it, the leg has somewhere to go and you can feel whether the
muscle actually engaged.
