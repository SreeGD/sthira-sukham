---
name: Reclining hand-to-big-toe pose
traditionalName: Supta Padangusthasana
tradition: Hatha yoga
modality: yoga
startPosition: supine
targets: [biceps-femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus, gastrocnemius]
goal: [mobility]
quickSteps:
  - Lie on your back, strap round one foot.
  - Raise that leg, knee softly bent.
  - Straighten only as far as the pelvis stays down.
  - Hold, then lower with a bent knee.
keyPoint: >-
  A straight leg is not the goal. A hamstring under gentle tension is.
feelItIn: >-
  The back of the raised thigh.
commonMistake: >-
  Straightening hard until the pelvis rolls back. Then it is a back stretch.
instructions:
  - Lie on your back with both legs extended along the floor.
  - Loop a strap around the arch of one foot and raise that leg toward the ceiling, keeping the knee softly bent to begin with.
  - Straighten the raised leg only as far as it goes without the pelvis rolling backward or the other leg lifting.
  - Hold, breathing steadily, then bend the knee and lower the leg under control.
dosage: Commonly held for 30 to 60 seconds per side in practice, repeated two or three times.
difficulty: beginner
progressions: [adho-mukha-svanasana]
contraindications:
  - Acute hamstring strain
  - Symptoms running down the leg that are reproduced by the position
  - Recent hip or knee surgery without clearance
stopIf:
  - Tingling, numbness, or shooting pain down the leg — that is nerve, not muscle, and stretching into it is the wrong response
  - Low back pain replaces the hamstring stretch
  - Sharp pain behind the knee
equipment: [strap, towel]
modifications:
  - Keep the raised knee bent throughout. A straight leg is not the goal — a hamstring under gentle tension is, and a bent knee delivers that perfectly well.
  - Bend the resting leg and put that foot flat on the floor if your low back lifts away from the floor.
  - Loop the strap around the ball of the foot rather than the toes if reaching the foot is uncomfortable.
props:
  - A belt, dressing-gown cord, or long towel works as well as a yoga strap.
  - A folded blanket under the head if the chin lifts toward the ceiling.
evidenceLabel: moderate-evidence
sources: [yoga-knee-oa-meta-2024, neumann-kinesiology-2016]
---

Mechanically this is a supine hamstring stretch with the pelvis stabilised by the floor,
which is what distinguishes it from stretching a hamstring while standing or seated. In
those positions the pelvis can tilt and quietly give back whatever range you thought you had
gained. Lying down, it cannot.

The strap does something worth understanding: it lets you keep the leg supported at the end
of the range without recruiting the hip flexors to hold it there. Holding the leg up with
muscle while trying to relax the back of it works against itself.

A note on the straight leg, because it causes more trouble than anything else here. The
straightness of the raised leg is not the point of the pose. If straightening it tips the
pelvis backward, the stretch has moved from your hamstring to your lower back, and you are
doing something other than what is described here.

A 2024 meta-analysis of eight randomised trials in people with knee osteoarthritis found
yoga programmes improved pain, stiffness, and physical function compared with controls,
though effects on daily activities and quality of life were less clear.
