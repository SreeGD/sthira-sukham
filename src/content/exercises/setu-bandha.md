  - Lie down, knees bent, feet hip-width.

  - Stop in a straight line, shoulders to knees.

  - label: Get into position
    detail: Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat, hip-width apart, heels a

  - label: Press through the heels
    detail: Press through your heels and lift your hips, starting from the tailbone and peeling the
      spine off the floor one part at a time.
  - label: Peel the spine up
    detail: Lift until your body makes a straight line from shoulders to knees, without arching the
      lower back further.
  - label: Lower one part at a time
    detail: Lower slowly, setting the spine down in reverse.

  reps: 8 to 12 repetitions

  frequency: Once daily
  note: Commonly described as 8 to 12 lifts, or held for 20 to 30 seconds, repeated two or three

  - Neck injury — do not turn the head while the hips are lifted

  - Any neck discomfort

  - none

  - Lift only a few inches. The height of the bridge is not what makes it work — the glutes

The bridge is one of the few ways to ask the gluteus maximus to extend the hip without also
demanding balance, and that makes it a reasonable starting point for the very common pattern
in which the hamstrings have quietly taken over the glutes' job.

Hamstring cramp during a bridge is genuinely informative rather than just annoying. It
usually means the hamstrings are doing work the glutes should be doing — which is the pattern
that leaves hamstrings short and busy, and short hamstrings resist the knee straightening
fully. Bringing the heels closer shortens the hamstrings' leverage and hands the work back.

Its relevance to a stiff knee is that a hip which extends properly is a hip that stops
borrowing from the knee.
