---
name: Pilates bridging
traditionalName: Shoulder Bridge
tradition: Pilates (mat repertoire)
modality: pilates
targets: [gluteus-maximus, biceps-femoris, semitendinosus]
goal: [strength, motor-control]
instructions:
  - Lie on your back with knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart.
  - Tilt the pelvis first, pressing the lower back gently toward the floor.
  - Peel the spine off the floor one vertebra at a time, from the tailbone upward, until you rest on your shoulder blades.
  - Reverse the sequence to come down, setting each part of the spine down in turn.
dosage: Commonly described as 6 to 10 slow repetitions.
difficulty: intermediate
regressions: [setu-bandha]
progressions: [pilates-side-lying-series]
contraindications:
  - Recent spinal surgery
  - Acute low back pain
  - Neck injury
stopIf:
  - Low back pain rather than work through the buttocks
  - Persistent hamstring cramp
  - Neck discomfort
equipment: [none]
modifications:
  - Lift only as far as feels controlled. Peeling off two or three segments of spine and returning slowly is the exercise; height is not.
  - Bring the heels closer to your buttocks if the hamstrings cramp, which shifts the work toward the glutes where it belongs.
props:
  - A folded blanket under the shoulders if the neck feels compressed.
evidenceLabel: mechanistic-rationale
sources: [neumann-kinesiology-2016, cochrane-exercise-knee-oa-2024]
---

The same shape as the yoga bridge with a different intention, and the difference is
instructive. The yoga version holds a position; this one is about the sequence of getting
there — articulating the spine one segment at a time in both directions.

That segmental control is the Pilates emphasis, and it makes the exercise harder while making
it lighter. Peeling down one part at a time is considerably more demanding than simply
lowering, and it exposes the places where the spine moves as a block rather than a chain.

For the knee, the relevant outcome is the same as the yoga bridge: a gluteus maximus that
extends the hip is a gluteus maximus that is not leaving the job to the hamstrings.
