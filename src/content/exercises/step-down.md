---
name: Step-down
modality: clinical-rom
startPosition: standing-supported
targets:
  - vastus-medialis
  - vastus-lateralis
  - gluteus-medius
  - gluteus-maximus
goal:
  - strength
  - motor-control
quickSteps:
  - Stand on a low step, one foot free.
  - Bend the standing knee slowly.
  - Touch the free heel down lightly.
  - Push back up. Repeat.
keyPoint: Lower slowly. The descent is the whole exercise.
feelItIn: The front of the standing thigh, and the side of that hip.
commonMistake: Starting too high. Begin with a single book.
instructions:
  - label: Get into position
    detail: Stand on a low step with one foot, the other foot free over the edge, holding a rail or
      wall for balance.
  - label: Lower slowly
    detail: Bend the standing knee slowly to lower the free heel toward the floor, keeping the knee
      tracking over the middle of the foot.
  - label: Touch down lightly
    detail: Touch the heel down lightly without transferring weight onto it.
  - label: Push back up
    detail: Push back up through the standing leg. The lowering is the part that matters — take it
      slowly.
dosage:
  reps: 10 to 15 repetitions
  sets: 1 to 3 sets per session
  frequency: Once daily
  note: Commonly described as 8 to 12 repetitions per side, one to three sets.
difficulty: advanced

  - pilates-standing-leg-pump
  - taichi-slow-flexion-extension
contraindications:
  - Front-of-knee pain on descending stairs
  - Balance problems without a secure rail
  - Recent knee surgery without clearance
stopIf:
  - Front-of-knee pain during the lowering
  - The knee drifts inward and you cannot correct it
  - The pelvis drops sharply on the free side
equipment:
  - chair
  - wall

Going downstairs is where stiff and painful knees most often complain, and this is that
movement isolated and slowed down.

The lowering phase asks the quadriceps to lengthen under load, which is both the harder
action and the one stairs actually demand. Pushing back up is almost incidental here.

Start with a step far lower than seems worth bothering with — a single book is a reasonable
first height. The quality that matters is the knee tracking over the foot and the pelvis
staying level, and both are lost long before the muscle runs out. When either fails, the
step is too high, regardless of how the thigh feels.
