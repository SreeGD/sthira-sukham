---
name: Step-down
modality: clinical-rom
startPosition: standing-supported
targets: [vastus-medialis, vastus-lateralis, gluteus-medius, gluteus-maximus]
goal: [strength, motor-control]
instructions:
  - Stand on a low step with one foot, the other foot free over the edge, holding a rail or wall for balance.
  - Bend the standing knee slowly to lower the free heel toward the floor, keeping the knee tracking over the middle of the foot.
  - Touch the heel down lightly without transferring weight onto it.
  - Push back up through the standing leg. The lowering is the part that matters — take it slowly.
dosage: Commonly described as 8 to 12 repetitions per side, one to three sets.
difficulty: advanced
regressions: [pilates-standing-leg-pump, taichi-slow-flexion-extension]
contraindications:
  - Front-of-knee pain on descending stairs
  - Balance problems without a secure rail
  - Recent knee surgery without clearance
stopIf:
  - Front-of-knee pain during the lowering
  - The knee drifts inward and you cannot correct it
  - The pelvis drops sharply on the free side
equipment: [chair, wall]
evidenceLabel: moderate-evidence
sources: [nascimento-hip-knee-pfp-2018, neumann-kinesiology-2016, cochrane-exercise-knee-oa-2024]
---

Going downstairs is where stiff and painful knees most often complain, and this is that
movement isolated and slowed down.

The lowering phase asks the quadriceps to lengthen under load, which is both the harder
action and the one stairs actually demand. Pushing back up is almost incidental here.

Start with a step far lower than seems worth bothering with — a single book is a reasonable
first height. The quality that matters is the knee tracking over the foot and the pelvis
staying level, and both are lost long before the muscle runs out. When either fails, the
step is too high, regardless of how the thigh feels.
