The deep squat is a resting position across much of the world and an athletic feat in much of
the rest, and the difference is habit rather than anatomy.

It is a useful diagnostic as much as an exercise, because it fails in informative ways. Heels
lifting points at the calf — soleus specifically, since the knee is bent. Knees collapsing
inward points at the hip. A back that rounds hard points at the hips too. The position asks
several things at once and tells you which one you are short of.

Depth is not the objective. Sitting to a cushion at whatever height you have available gives
you the same movement, and going deeper than your hips and ankles allow simply transfers the
demand into the knee joint, which is the one part of this you were trying to help.
