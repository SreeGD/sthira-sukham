quickSteps:
  - Lie face down, shins off the end of the bed.
  - Let the legs hang. Do not push.
  - Rest there and breathe.
  - Come off slowly.

feelItIn: A gentle pull behind the knee as it settles toward straight.
commonMistake: Adding weight too early. If you want it to end, the load is already too high.
instructions:
  - label: Get into position
    detail: Lie face down on a bed or bench with your thighs supported and both shins hanging off
      the end from the knees down.
  - label: Let the legs hang
    detail: Let the legs hang. Do not push, pull, or bounce — the weight of the lower leg is the
      entire load.
  - label: Keep the thigh supported
    detail: Keep the thigh in contact with the surface so the movement happens at the knee.
  - label: Rest and breathe
    detail: Rest there and breathe. Time under a gentle load is what does the work here.

  reps: 1 repetition
  sets: 1 set per session
  frequency: Once daily
  hold: 2 to 10 minutes
  note: Commonly described as 5 to 10 minutes, once or twice a day. Some protocols add a light

  - The knee is more painful an hour later than when you started

Straightening gets less attention than bending, and it is often the more consequential loss.
A knee that will not fully straighten means the quadriceps has to work during every second of
standing, because the joint never reaches the locked position where the skeleton holds you up
for free.

The prone hang is deliberately passive. Gravity applies a very small load for a long time,
which is how passive tissue actually responds — sustained gentle tension rather than forceful
brief stretching.

It is dull, and it works. If it is uncomfortable enough that you want it to be over, the load
is too high: shorten the time before you add anything.
