  - Recent knee or hip surgery without clearance

  - A twisting sensation in the knee

modifications:
  - Support the front hip generously with a firm cushion or folded blanket. An unsupported hip
    lets the body weight rotate through the front knee, which is how this pose injures people.

Included with more caution than anything else in this library, because it is both very common
in yoga classes and a well-recognised way to hurt a knee.

The mechanics are the problem. The front leg is bent and rotated with body weight above it,
and if the hip will not rotate far enough, the difference has to come from somewhere — the
knee, which has very little rotation to give. That is why the front hip must be supported: an
unsupported hip drops, the pelvis twists, and the leverage arrives at the knee joint.

For most people reading a knee reference, the supine version with the ankle crossed over the
opposite thigh is the right choice. It reaches the same muscles, it is easy to control, and it
asks nothing of the knee. This entry describes the floor version because people meet it in
classes and deserve to know what makes it risky, not because it is the version to aim for.
