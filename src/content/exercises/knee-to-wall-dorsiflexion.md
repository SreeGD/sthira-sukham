---
name: Knee-to-wall ankle mobilisation
modality: clinical-rom
startPosition: standing-supported
targets:
  - ankle-capsule
  - soleus
goal:
  - mobility
quickSteps:
  - Stand facing a wall, hands on it, one foot forward.
  - Keep that heel glued down.
  - Drive the knee forward until it touches the wall.
  - Return, then edge the foot back and repeat.
keyPoint: The heel stays down. The moment it lifts, the movement stops being the ankle's.
feelItIn: A deep stretch at the back of the ankle and low in the calf — not at the front of
  the joint.
commonMistake: Letting the heel peel up to get the knee further forward, which measures
  nothing and stretches nothing.
instructions:
  - label: Set up facing the wall
    detail: Stand an arm's length from a wall and place both hands on it at shoulder height.
  - label: Choose the foot and place it
    detail: Step the stiffer ankle forward — if neither is worse, start with the left and do
      both. Point the foot straight at the wall, toes about a hand's width from it.
  - label: Anchor the heel
    detail: Press that heel firmly into the floor. It stays down for every repetition; this
      is the whole point of the exercise.
  - label: Drive the knee forward
    detail: Bend the front knee and travel it straight forward towards the wall, tracking
      over the middle toes rather than falling inward.
  - label: Touch and return
    detail: Go until the knee touches the wall or until the heel starts to lift, whichever
      comes first, then return to the start.
  - label: Find your working distance
    detail: If the knee touches easily, slide the foot back a centimetre and repeat. If the
      heel lifts before contact, slide the foot forward. Work where the heel just barely stays.
  - label: Change sides
    detail: Repeat the whole sequence with the other foot forward, and note whether the two
      sides differ.
dosage:
  reps: 10 repetitions per side
  sets: 1 to 3 sets per session
  frequency: Once daily, or twice on days you sit a lot
  note: Short repeated visits to the end of range suit capsular tissue better than one long hold.
difficulty: beginner
progressions:
  - single-leg-balance
contraindications:
  - Recent ankle fracture or surgery without clearance
  - Sharp pinching pain at the front of the ankle rather than a stretch behind it
stopIf:
  - Pinching at the front of the ankle joint
  - Pain rather than stretch at the back of the heel
  - The ankle feeling unstable or as though it might give way
equipment:
  - wall
evidenceLabel: mechanistic-rationale
sources:
  - neumann-kinesiology-2016
laterality: unilateral
---

This is the exercise that tells you *which* ankle problem you have, which is why it is worth
doing even before it is worth repeating.

Do it once with the knee bent, as described. Then stand with the leg straight and lean into
the same wall to stretch the calf. **If the bent-knee version gives you noticeably more range,
your restriction is gastrocnemius**, the calf muscle that crosses the knee — bending the knee
takes it out of the equation. **If both versions stop at the same point, the restriction is
not muscular**; it is the joint itself, and stretching harder will not shift it.

That distinction changes what helps. A short gastrocnemius responds to stretching held for
time. A tight capsule responds to being taken to the end of its range repeatedly and briefly —
which is what this exercise is.

The heel discipline is not fussiness. Once the heel lifts, the shin is no longer travelling
over a fixed foot, and the ankle has stopped moving altogether.

Dorsiflexion is the range people lose without noticing, because walking on the flat asks for
very little of it. It is missed on stairs, on hills and in squatting — often years after the
event that caused it.
