name: Banded terminal knee extension
modality: clinical-rom
startPosition: standing-supported
targets: [vastus-medialis, vastus-lateralis, vastus-intermedius]
goal: [strength, motor-control]
quickSteps:
  - Band behind the knee, anchored in front.
  - Step back until the band is taut.
  - Straighten the knee against the band.
  - Let it bend back slowly.
keyPoint: >-
  The band pulls the knee into a bend; you straighten against it.
feelItIn: >-
  The thigh just above the kneecap.
commonMistake: >-
  A weak anchor. A band that slips travels fast at knee height.
instructions:
  - Loop a resistance band around a solid anchor at knee height and step into it so the band sits in the crease behind your knee.
  - Step back until the band is taut, pulling the knee gently forward into a slight bend.
  - Stand with that foot flat and straighten the knee against the band, tightening the thigh.
  - Hold the straight position briefly, then let the band draw the knee slowly back into the bend.
dosage: Commonly described as 10 to 15 repetitions, two or three sets, once daily.

contraindications:
  - Recent knee surgery where your surgeon has restricted active straightening
  - Front-of-knee pain on straightening
  - No solid anchor point — a band that slips loose snaps back hard
stopIf:
  - Front-of-knee pain during the straightening
  - The knee is pulled further into the bend than you can control
  - Any catching or clunking

The standing version of terminal knee extension, with a band supplying resistance where the
towel-roll version supplies only position.

What the band adds is a load that increases as the knee straightens. That matches the
problem: the last fifteen degrees of extension are where the quadriceps has least mechanical
advantage and where it is weakest after any period of swelling, so meeting them with the
most resistance is the point rather than an accident.

The anchor matters more than the band. A band that comes loose under tension travels fast
and at knee height. Use something that will not move.
