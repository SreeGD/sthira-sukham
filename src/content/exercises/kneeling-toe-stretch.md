instructions:
  - label: Kneel on a padded surface
    detail: Kneel on a mat or folded blanket. Both knees stay down; this position works both feet
      at once.
  - label: Tuck the toes
    detail: Curl all ten toes forward so the balls of the feet are on the mat and the heels point
      up. Check that the big toes are pointing straight back, not rolling outward.
  - label: Take weight in your hands
    detail: Place your hands on the floor in front of you and lean into them, so you control
      exactly how much weight the feet carry.
  - label: Sit back a little
    detail: Move your hips back towards your heels only as far as gives a strong stretch you can
      breathe through. For many people that is a few centimetres, not the whole way.
  - label: Hold and breathe
    detail: Stay for 20 to 30 seconds, breathing steadily. Ease forward onto your hands whenever
      it becomes too much rather than enduring it.
  - label: Come out and reverse it
    detail: Lift the hips, untuck the toes, and sit back briefly with the tops of the feet flat to
      stretch the front of the ankle the other way.

  reps: 2 to 3 holds of 20 to 30 seconds
  sets: 1 set per session
  frequency: 3 to 4 times a week
  note: Both feet work at once, so there is no second side to do.
difficulty: intermediate
regressions:
  - big-toe-extension-mobilisation
contraindications:
  - Knee pain in kneeling, or a knee that will not tolerate weight
  - A hot, red or visibly swollen big toe joint
  - Recent forefoot or toe fracture without clearance
  - Numbness in the feet, or reduced sensation from any cause
stopIf:
  - Sharp pain in the big toe joint rather than a stretch beneath it
  - Cramping in the arch that does not settle
  - Pins and needles or the foot going numb
  - Knee pain of any kind
equipment:
  - cushion
props:
  - A folded blanket under the knees.
  - A cushion between the heels and the hips to reduce how far you sit back.

  - Keep most of your weight in your hands. This is the main adjustment and it makes the pose
    accessible to almost everyone.
  - Place a cushion between your heels and hips so you cannot sit back too far.
  - If one foot is much stiffer, untuck the easier side and work one foot at a time.

This is the position where most people first discover their big toe has stopped bending.

It is a traditional kneeling posture used for sitting, and in its toes-tucked form it asks for
close to full big-toe extension while carrying body weight. That combination makes it an honest
test as much as a stretch: if you cannot sit back, it is worth knowing whether the limit is the
toes, the ankles or the knees, because they are different problems.

**It is intense, and the intensity is why people abandon it.** The fix is simple and often
skipped: keep your hands on the floor and let them carry most of your weight. That turns an
all-or-nothing posture into one you can dial to any level, including a very small one.

There is a reason this matters beyond the pose. Seated meditation postures and long kneeling both
depend on this range, and a big toe that will not extend is a common and rarely-identified reason
that sitting becomes uncomfortable after a few minutes.

Always follow it by sitting briefly with the tops of the feet flat. The feet have just been held
at one end of their range, and going the other way afterwards settles them.
