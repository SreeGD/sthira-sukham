name: Short foot
modality: clinical-rom
startPosition: seated
targets:
  - intrinsic-foot-muscles
goal:
  - strength
  - motor-control
quickSteps:
  - Sit with the foot flat on the floor.
  - Without curling the toes, draw the ball of the foot towards the heel.
  - The arch lifts a few millimetres. Hold.
  - Release slowly and repeat.
keyPoint: The toes stay long and flat. If they curl, the wrong muscles are doing it.
feelItIn: A deep, unfamiliar cramping-like effort inside the arch itself.
commonMistake: Gripping with the toes, which uses the long muscles from the lower leg — exactly
  the ones already doing too much.
instructions:
  - label: Sit with the foot flat
    detail: Sit on a chair with both feet flat on the floor, knees roughly over the ankles, shoes off.
  - label: Choose the foot
    detail: Start with the stiffer or flatter foot if one is worse; otherwise start with the left
      and do both.
  - label: Spread the toes first
    detail: Lift and spread all five toes, then lay them back down flat and long. They stay that
      way for the rest of the repetition.
  - label: Shorten the foot
    detail: Without curling the toes or lifting the heel, gently draw the ball of the foot back
      towards the heel, as though shortening the footprint.
  - label: Notice the arch
    detail: The arch should rise a few millimetres. It is a small movement — if it is large, the
      toes are probably helping.
  - label: Hold, then release
    detail: Hold for around five seconds, breathing normally, then release slowly and let the foot
      settle before the next repetition.
  - label: Change sides
    detail: Repeat the whole set with the other foot.
dosage:
  reps: 8 to 10 holds of about 5 seconds per side
  sets: 1 to 2 sets per session
  frequency: Once daily
  note: Progress by doing it standing, then in a single-leg stance, once it is reliable seated.

progressions:
  - single-leg-balance
contraindications:
  - Acute plantar heel pain severe enough that light arch effort hurts
  - Recent foot surgery without clearance
stopIf:
  - Cramp in the arch that does not settle when you stop
  - Sharp pain under the heel
  - Pins and needles in the toes
equipment:
  - none

This is the hardest easy exercise in the library. There is almost nothing to see, the movement is
a few millimetres, and most people cannot do it at all for the first week.

The reason it is worth the frustration: the arch has two support systems. The plantar fascia holds
it up passively, and the small muscles inside the foot hold it up actively. When the muscular half
stops contributing, the passive half carries everything — and passive tissue does not adapt to
overload, it just gets irritated.

**The toes are the tell.** If they curl, you are using the long muscles that run down from the
lower leg, which are the ones already compensating. Toes long and flat, arch lifting quietly
underneath: that is the small muscles inside the foot, working.

Expect it to feel like the beginning of a cramp. That sensation is normal in a muscle group that
has rarely been asked to contract on purpose, and it settles within a couple of weeks.

Do not judge progress by how high the arch lifts. Judge it by whether you can produce the lift
without the toes joining in.
