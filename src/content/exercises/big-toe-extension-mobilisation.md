quickSteps:
  - Sit and rest one ankle across the opposite knee.
  - Hold the foot steady with one hand.
  - With the other, bend the big toe upward to a firm stop.
  - Hold briefly, release, repeat.
keyPoint: Hold the foot itself still. If the whole foot moves, the toe joint is not being asked
  for anything.
feelItIn: A stretch underneath the big toe and along the sole towards the heel.
commonMistake: Bending the toe at its middle joint instead of at the base, which stretches the toe
  rather than the joint that matters.
instructions:
  - label: Sit and cross the ankle
    detail: Sit on a chair and rest one ankle across the opposite knee so the sole faces inward
      and you can reach it comfortably.
  - label: Choose the foot
    detail: Start with the stiffer big toe if one is worse; otherwise start with the left and do
      both.
  - label: Anchor the foot
    detail: Grip the middle of the foot firmly with one hand so it cannot move. This hand does not
      let go during the repetition.
  - label: Find the right joint
    detail: With the other hand, take hold of the big toe near its base — the joint where the toe
      meets the ball of the foot, not the knuckle further out.
  - label: Bend the toe upward
    detail: Draw the big toe up towards the top of the foot until you reach a firm stop. Go to
      firm resistance, never to pain.
  - label: Hold and release
    detail: Hold for around 20 seconds, then release slowly and let the toe rest before repeating.
  - label: Change sides
    detail: Repeat with the other foot, and note whether one toe travels noticeably less.
dosage:
  reps: 3 to 5 holds of about 20 seconds per side
  sets: 1 set per session
  frequency: Once daily
  note: Bending at the base joint is what counts. A big range at the outer knuckle achieves nothing.

  - kneeling-toe-stretch

  - A hot, red or visibly swollen big toe joint
  - Recent forefoot fracture or surgery without clearance
  - A hard bony block that stops the toe abruptly, rather than a stretch
stopIf:
  - Sharp pain in the joint rather than a stretch beneath it
  - Pain that lingers after you let go
  - Numbness or tingling in the toe

  - none

Walking asks the big toe to bend upward about 65° on every step. It is a lot of range from a small
joint, and it is asked for thousands of times a day.

When it is not available, the body finds it elsewhere — usually by rolling off the inner edge of
the foot instead of over the toe. That turns the foot in, turns the shin in with it, and changes
the angle the knee above is loaded at. This is the chain running one joint further down than usual.

**Getting the right joint matters more than the size of the stretch.** The one that counts is at
the base of the toe, where the toe meets the ball of the foot. It is easy to bend the knuckle
further out instead, which feels like something and achieves nothing.

Pay attention to how the movement ends. A stretch that builds gradually and eases is soft tissue.
A stop that is hard and abrupt, as though something is in the way, is a different problem — and
forcing it is not the answer. That end feel, especially with a bump on the top of the joint, is
worth having looked at rather than worked on.
