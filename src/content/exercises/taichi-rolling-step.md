quickSteps:
  - Stand tall, weight on one leg.
  - Place the other heel down softly, with no weight on it.
  - Roll the weight forward along the foot, heel to toe.
  - Transfer fully, then step again.
keyPoint: The foot is placed empty and filled afterwards. Landing with weight already on it
  turns this back into ordinary walking.
feelItIn: The whole sole in sequence, and steady work in the standing leg's hip and arch.
commonMistake: Going too fast. At walking speed the roll disappears and the foot lands flat.
instructions:
  - label: Stand and settle
    detail: Stand with the feet about hip-width apart, knees soft rather than locked, weight even.
  - label: Shift fully onto one leg
    detail: Move your weight over the left leg until the right foot is genuinely empty. Take your
      time; this is the part people rush.
  - label: Place the empty heel
    detail: Step the right foot forward and set the heel down softly with no weight through it.
      It should be silent.
  - label: Roll the weight forward
    detail: Slowly transfer your weight along the right foot — heel, then outer edge, then the
      ball, then the big toe — as though painting the floor with the sole.
  - label: Arrive and pause
    detail: When the weight is fully over the right foot, pause and feel the left foot become
      empty in turn.
  - label: Continue, alternating
    detail: Step with the left foot the same way. Both feet get equal work as you continue, so
      there is no separate second side.
dosage:
  reps: 10 to 20 steps
  sets: 1 to 2 sets per session
  frequency: 3 to 4 times a week
  hold: Around 3 seconds per step
  note: Slower is harder. If it feels easy, halve the speed rather than adding steps.
difficulty: intermediate
regressions:
  - taichi-heel-toe-walking
contraindications:
  - Balance problems or a history of falls without supervision or something to hold
  - Dizziness on standing or turning
  - Numbness or reduced sensation in the feet
stopIf:
  - Loss of balance, or needing to grab for support repeatedly
  - Dizziness
  - Cramp in the arch that does not settle

  - none

  - A wall or chair back within reach for the first few sessions.

  - Keep one hand lightly on a wall or chair back until the slow transfer feels secure.
  - Shorten the step considerably. A small step done slowly is worth more than a long one rushed.
  - If the big toe is painful, roll to the ball of the foot and stop short of the toe.

Tai chi walking is ordinary walking taken apart and done deliberately, and the foot is where most
of the difference shows.

In everyday walking the foot lands already loaded and the sole functions largely as one block. Here
the foot is placed **empty** and filled afterwards, which forces the weight to travel across it in
sequence — heel, outer edge, ball, big toe. That sequence is exactly the one a stiff foot has
stopped performing, and doing it slowly makes any gap in it obvious.

The traditional name is the cat walk, after the soft, weightless quality of the placement. That
image is a better instruction than any anatomical description: a cat puts its foot down before
deciding to trust it.

**Speed is the whole difficulty.** At normal walking pace the roll happens too quickly to feel or
to control, and the foot reverts to landing flat. Three seconds a step is a reasonable target, and
slower is harder rather than easier.

There is a second benefit that has nothing to do with the moving foot. Every step spends several
seconds balanced on the other leg, which asks the standing foot's arch and the hip above it to
work continuously — considerably more demanding than the brief single-leg moments of ordinary
walking.
