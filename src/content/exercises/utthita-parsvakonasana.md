Warrior II with a side bend added, which loads the front leg for longer while asking the back
leg's inner thigh to lengthen.

It is included as an advanced pose deliberately. Everything that can go wrong in Warrior II
can go wrong here, with the additional temptation to reach the lower hand to the floor —
which for most people means dropping the chest forward, rotating the pelvis, and letting the
front knee cave in to get there.

The forearm-on-thigh version is what most practitioners should be doing most of the time. It
keeps the chest open and the knee where it belongs, and it is the version that does what the
pose is for.
