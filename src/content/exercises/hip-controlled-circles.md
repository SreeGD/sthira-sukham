name: Controlled hip circles
modality: clinical-rom
startPosition: quadruped
targets:
  - hip-capsule
  - deep-hip-rotators
  - gluteus-medius
goal:
  - mobility
  - motor-control
quickSteps:
  - On hands and knees, lift one knee out to the side.
  - Carry it slowly backward in a wide arc.
  - Bring it back the same way.
  - Keep the back and pelvis still throughout.
keyPoint: Slow and small beats fast and big. The moment your back starts moving, the hip has
  stopped.
feelItIn: Deep in the hip socket, and as effort in the standing-side buttock holding you level.
commonMistake: Swinging the leg so far that the pelvis rotates — which moves the spine and
  leaves the hip doing very little.
instructions:
  - label: Get onto hands and knees
    detail: Hands under shoulders, knees under hips, back flat and level like a tabletop.
  - label: Choose the side and set the pelvis
    detail: Start with the stiffer hip if one is worse; otherwise start with the left and do
      both. Brace gently through the middle so the pelvis will not tip or twist.
  - label: Lift the knee out to the side
    detail: Keeping the knee bent, lift it away from the floor and out to the side, as though
      drawing a small arc with it. Go only as far as you can without the back moving.
  - label: Carry it backward
    detail: From there, travel the knee slowly backward and around behind you, staying at the
      edge of the range where you still have control.
  - label: Reverse the same path
    detail: Bring the knee back the way it came and set it down. That is one repetition.
  - label: Change sides
    detail: Repeat with the other leg, and note whether one hip travels less than the other.
dosage:
  reps: 5 to 8 slow circles per side
  sets: 1 to 2 sets per session
  frequency: 3 to 4 times a week
  note: Each circle should take several seconds. Rushing turns it into a swing.
difficulty: intermediate
regressions:
  - pilates-clam
contraindications:
  - Wrist or shoulder pain that prevents bearing weight on the hands
  - Pinching or catching deep in the groin at the end of the range
  - Recent hip surgery or hip replacement without clearance
stopIf:
  - A pinch or catch at the front of the groin
  - Clicking that is painful rather than merely audible
  - Back pain, which means the movement has moved to the spine
equipment:
  - cushion

  - neumann-kinesiology-2016

Capsular tissue does not respond to strengthening and it does not respond well to a long
static hold. What it responds to is being taken to the edge of its range, under control, and
returned — repeatedly.

That is unusual enough to be worth saying plainly: **this is not a stretch.** You are not
trying to reach further today than yesterday. You are asking the hip to travel its own
existing range actively, which is what keeps that range available.

Most people find they have far less controlled range than passive range. The hip that
happily flops out to the side when someone else pushes it will often move a fraction of that
distance when you have to move it yourself without help from the spine. That gap is the
thing worth closing.

The pelvis staying still is what makes it work. It is also what makes it much harder than it
looks, and why a small circle done honestly beats a large one done by swinging.

If you feel a pinch at the front of the groin rather than a stretch deep in the socket, stop.
That is a different problem from a tight capsule, and pushing into it is the wrong response.
