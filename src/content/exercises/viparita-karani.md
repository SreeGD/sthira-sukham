---
name: Legs up the wall
traditionalName: Viparita Karani
tradition: Hatha yoga
modality: yoga
startPosition: supine
targets: [biceps-femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus, joint-capsule]
goal: [mobility]
instructions:
  - Sit sideways with one hip against a wall, then swing your legs up the wall as you lie back.
  - Shuffle your hips as close to the wall as is comfortable — they do not need to touch it.
  - Let the legs rest against the wall with the knees softly bent rather than locked.
  - Rest and breathe. Come out by bending the knees and rolling to one side.
dosage: Commonly held for 5 to 15 minutes.
difficulty: beginner
progressions: [supta-padangusthasana]
contraindications:
  - Glaucoma or uncontrolled high blood pressure, given the legs-above-heart position
  - Acute low back pain that this position reproduces
  - Any condition where lying flat is uncomfortable
stopIf:
  - Pins and needles in the feet that do not settle when you bend the knees
  - Low back pain
  - Head pressure or dizziness
equipment: [wall, cushion]
modifications:
  - Keep the knees clearly bent. Straight legs are not the goal and a locked knee is the opposite of what this is for.
  - Move the hips further from the wall. Even a foot away is a legitimate version and much easier on the hamstrings.
  - Place a folded blanket under the hips if the low back is uncomfortable.
props:
  - A folded blanket for under the hips, and one under the head.
evidenceLabel: traditional-practice
sources: [yoga-knee-oa-meta-2024, neumann-kinesiology-2016]
---

The gentlest position in this library, and the one most likely to be available on a bad day.

Two things are happening. The hamstrings are held at a mild length for a long time, which
is how passive tissue actually responds — sustained low load rather than brief force. And
the leg is above the heart, which is the standard positional advice for a knee that is
puffy at the end of the day.

The distance from the wall is the entire adjustment. Hips against the wall is a strong
hamstring stretch; hips a foot back is barely a stretch at all. Most people should start
much further back than they expect and move in over weeks, not minutes.
