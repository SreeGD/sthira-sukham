---
name: Downward-facing dog
traditionalName: Adho Mukha Svanasana
tradition: Hatha yoga
modality: yoga
startPosition: quadruped
targets: [gastrocnemius, biceps-femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus]
goal: [mobility]
instructions:
  - Start on hands and knees, hands slightly ahead of your shoulders.
  - Tuck your toes and lift your hips upward and backward, keeping your knees generously bent to begin with.
  - Lengthen your spine first. Only once your back is long, begin to ease the knees toward straight and the heels toward the floor.
  - Hold, then lower the knees back to the floor.
dosage: Commonly held for 30 seconds to a minute, repeated two or three times.
difficulty: intermediate
regressions: [supta-padangusthasana]
contraindications:
  - Wrist injury or carpal tunnel symptoms
  - Shoulder injury
  - High blood pressure not under control, or glaucoma, given the inverted head position
  - Recent knee surgery without clearance
stopIf:
  - Wrist pain
  - Pain behind the knee rather than a stretch
  - Dizziness or head pressure
equipment: [chair, wall]
modifications:
  - Keep the knees bent throughout. Straight legs are not the goal — a long spine is, and most people get more from this pose with the knees bent than straight.
  - Do it with your hands on a chair seat or a wall instead of the floor. This removes most of the load from the wrists and shoulders and makes the position far more sustainable.
  - Take the heels off the floor entirely. Pressing them down before the calves are ready just rounds the back.
props:
  - A chair or wall for the hands.
  - A folded mat under the wrists if they are sensitive.
evidenceLabel: traditional-practice
sources: [yoga-knee-oa-meta-2024, neumann-kinesiology-2016]
---

Downward dog reaches the whole back line of the leg at once — hamstrings and calves together
— which is efficient and also the reason it is so often done in a way that reaches none of
them properly.

The order matters. Lengthen the spine first with the knees bent, then straighten the knees
gradually. Reverse that order and the pelvis tucks under, the back rounds, and the hamstrings
never get near their end range no matter how straight the legs look.

The chair version deserves more use than it gets. Most of what makes this pose difficult is
carrying body weight through the wrists and shoulders, which has nothing to do with the leg
stretch you came for. Hands on a chair keeps the leg mechanics and discards the rest.
