Downward dog reaches the whole back line of the leg at once — hamstrings and calves together
— which is efficient and also the reason it is so often done in a way that reaches none of
them properly.

The order matters. Lengthen the spine first with the knees bent, then straighten the knees
gradually. Reverse that order and the pelvis tucks under, the back rounds, and the hamstrings
never get near their end range no matter how straight the legs look.

The chair version deserves more use than it gets. Most of what makes this pose difficult is
carrying body weight through the wrists and shoulders, which has nothing to do with the leg
stretch you came for. Hands on a chair keeps the leg mechanics and discards the rest.
