---
name: Clam
traditionalName: Clam
tradition: Pilates (mat repertoire)
modality: pilates
startPosition: side-lying
targets: [gluteus-medius, gluteus-minimus, tensor-fasciae-latae]
goal: [strength, motor-control]
instructions:
  - Lie on your side with your hips stacked, knees bent to about a right angle and heels in line with your back.
  - Rest your head on your lower arm and place your top hand on the floor in front for balance.
  - Keeping your heels touching and your pelvis completely still, lift the top knee.
  - Lower slowly. If your top hip rolls backward, the lift has gone past your range.
dosage: Commonly described as 10 to 15 repetitions per side, one to two sets.
difficulty: beginner
progressions: [pilates-side-lying-series]
contraindications:
  - Recent hip surgery without clearance
  - Pinching at the front or side of the hip in this position
stopIf:
  - Pinching at the side of the hip
  - Low back pain
  - The pelvis rolls back and you cannot stop it even over a small range
equipment: [cushion]
modifications:
  - Lift only an inch or two. The pelvis staying still is the exercise; the height of the knee is not.
  - Place your top hand on your hip bone so you can feel the moment the pelvis starts to roll.
  - Put a cushion between your head and arm so the neck is not held in a side bend.
props:
  - A cushion for under the head, and one between the knees if the hips are uncomfortable.
evidenceLabel: moderate-evidence
sources: [nascimento-hip-knee-pfp-2018, neumann-kinesiology-2016]

The most commonly prescribed gluteus medius exercise there is, and one of the most commonly
done wrong.

The fault is always the same: the pelvis rolls backward, which lets the top knee travel much
further and hands most of the work to the muscles at the front of the hip. It feels like
progress because the range increased. It is not, because the range came from somewhere else.

A hand on the hip bone is worth more than a mirror here. You will feel the roll begin
before you see it, and the point just before it is your actual working range.

Its relevance to a stiff knee runs through the hip: a meta-analysis of 14 trials found hip
and knee strengthening together outperformed knee strengthening alone for patellofemoral
pain.
