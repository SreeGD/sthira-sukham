The most commonly prescribed gluteus medius exercise there is, and one of the most commonly
done wrong.

The fault is always the same: the pelvis rolls backward, which lets the top knee travel much
further and hands most of the work to the muscles at the front of the hip. It feels like
progress because the range increased. It is not, because the range came from somewhere else.

A hand on the hip bone is worth more than a mirror here. You will feel the roll begin
before you see it, and the point just before it is your actual working range.

Its relevance to a stiff knee runs through the hip: a meta-analysis of 14 trials found hip
and knee strengthening together outperformed knee strengthening alone for patellofemoral
pain.
