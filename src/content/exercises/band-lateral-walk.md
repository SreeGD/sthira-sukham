---
name: Banded lateral walk
modality: clinical-rom
startPosition: standing
targets: [gluteus-medius, gluteus-minimus, tensor-fasciae-latae]
goal: [strength, motor-control]
quickSteps:
  - Loop a band above both knees.
  - Soft knees, push them out against the band.
  - Step sideways, keeping tension.
  - Bring the other foot to follow.
keyPoint: >-
  The knees stay pushed outward the whole time.
feelItIn: >-
  The side of the hip, burning within a few steps.
commonMistake: >-
  Letting the knees cave in, which trains the exact fault this fixes.
instructions:
  - Place a looped resistance band around both legs, just above the knees to begin with.
  - Stand with feet hip-width apart, knees softly bent, and push the knees gently outward against the band.
  - Step sideways with one foot, keeping tension on the band, then bring the other foot to follow — without letting the feet touch.
  - Continue in one direction, then reverse. Keep the torso upright and the knees pointing forward.
dosage: Commonly described as 10 to 15 steps in each direction, one to three sets.
difficulty: intermediate
regressions: [pilates-clam, band-clam]
progressions: [step-down]
contraindications:
  - Knee pain on partial squatting
  - Balance problems without something within reach
  - Acute hip or groin pain
stopIf:
  - Knee pain
  - The knees collapse inward against the band and you cannot correct it
  - Pinching at the side of the hip
equipment: [resistance-band]
evidenceLabel: moderate-evidence
sources: [nascimento-hip-knee-pfp-2018, neumann-kinesiology-2016]
---

Gluteus medius worked standing and under load, which is closer to its actual job than
anything done lying down.

Band placement is the dial. Above the knees is easiest and the usual starting point; moving
the band to the ankles increases the demand substantially, because the lever is longer. Move
it down only when the knees stay pointing forward for the whole set.

The fault to watch is the knees caving inward — which is the exact pattern this exercise
exists to correct, so doing it while it happens trains the wrong thing. When you cannot hold
the knees out, the set is over regardless of how many steps you had planned.
