  - Lie down, feet on a wall, knees bent.

  - Press part-way straight.

  - label: Align the knees
    detail: Check that your knees point in the same direction as your second toe, and keep them there.
  - label: Press part-way straight
    detail: Press through your feet to straighten your legs part-way, sliding your body slightly
      away from the wall.
  - label: Bend back slowly
    detail: Bend slowly back to the start, controlling the return rather than collapsing into it.
      Watch that the knees do not drift inward. Complete your repetitions on this leg, then
      repeat with the other.

Reformer footwork adapted to a wall. The reformer version has a spring providing resistance;
this version uses a fraction of body weight, which for a stiff or irritable knee is often the
right amount anyway.

The value is in the position rather than the load. Lying down means the knee is loaded without
carrying you, and the alignment of the knee over the foot is directly visible the whole time —
which is exactly the thing that goes wrong under load and is hardest to notice while standing.

Slowness is not decoration. The return is where control is trained, and rushing it discards
most of what the exercise offers.
