A bike takes the knee through its bending range repeatedly, under almost no load, with the
body weight supported. That combination is hard to get any other way, which is why it turns
up in nearly every knee rehabilitation programme.

The saddle height is the dial, and it is worth understanding rather than guessing. A high
saddle demands less knee bend per revolution; lowering it demands more. Starting high and
coming down over minutes or weeks is a way of asking for slightly more range each time
without ever forcing it.

Rocking back and forth is a legitimate substitute when a full revolution is not yet
available — a knee that cannot complete a circle can still visit both ends of what it has.
