---
name: Slow loaded bending and straightening
traditionalName: Song Yao (slow sinking practice)
tradition: Tai chi (foundational stance practice)
modality: taichi-qigong
startPosition: standing
targets:
  - rectus-femoris
  - vastus-lateralis
  - vastus-medialis
  - vastus-intermedius
  - popliteus
goal:
  - strength
  - motor-control
quickSteps:
  - Stand, feet shoulder-width.
  - Sink slowly by bending both knees.
  - Take at least five seconds down.
  - Rise just as slowly, no locking out.
keyPoint: The slow descent is the valuable half.
feelItIn: The front of the thighs working continuously.
commonMistake: Going deeper than you can control. Depth is the dial; use it.
instructions:
  - label: Set your stance
    detail: Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, hands resting easily at your sides or in front of
      you.
  - label: Sink slowly
    detail: Sink slowly by bending both knees, as though lowering into a very high chair. Keep your
      torso upright and your weight over the middle of your feet.
  - label: Choose your depth
    detail: Take at least five seconds to descend, and go only as deep as stays completely comfortable.
  - label: Rise just as slowly
    detail: Rise just as slowly, without locking out sharply at the top. Keep breathing throughout.
dosage:
  reps: 8 to 12 slow cycles
  sets: 1 to 2 sets per session
  frequency: Once daily
  note: Commonly practised as 8 to 12 slow cycles, or continuously for 2 to 3 minutes.
difficulty: intermediate
regressions:
  - taichi-knee-over-toe
contraindications:
  - Knee pain on partial squatting
  - Recent knee surgery without clearance
  - Balance problems without support nearby
stopIf:
  - Front-of-knee pain during the descent
  - Trembling you cannot control — that means the depth is beyond what you can currently manage
  - Any catching or locking
equipment:
  - none
evidenceLabel: moderate-evidence
sources:
  - wang-taichi-knee-oa-2016
  - neumann-kinesiology-2016
  - cochrane-exercise-knee-oa-2024
laterality: bilateral
---

Slow controlled bending under body weight, which trains the quadriceps through a range rather
than at a single point — and does it without impact, without momentum, and at a load you set
by choosing your depth.

The descent is the valuable half. Lowering slowly asks the quadriceps to lengthen while
working, which is both the more demanding action and the one most relevant to the situations
where stiff knees give trouble: going downstairs, sitting down, walking downhill.

Popliteus is listed among the targets because the transition out of a fully straight knee is
where it does its work, and this practice passes through that transition slowly and repeatedly
rather than skipping past it.

The self-regulating quality is what makes it well suited to an irritable knee. Depth is the
dial, it is continuously adjustable, and nothing about the exercise pushes you deeper than you
chose to go.
