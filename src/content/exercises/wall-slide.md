name: Wall slide

startPosition: supine
targets:
  - rectus-femoris
  - vastus-medialis

goal:
  - mobility
quickSteps:
  - Lie on your back with a leg up the wall.
  - Let the heel slide slowly down.
  - Rest at a firm stretch.
  - Push the heel back up with your hands.
keyPoint: Let gravity bend the knee. If you are gripping, nothing is stretching.
feelItIn: A stretch at the front of the knee.
commonMistake: Tensing the thigh to control the slide, which fights the stretch.
instructions:
  - label: Get into position
    detail: Lie on your back close to a wall, with the leg you are working resting up against it,
      heel on the wall.
  - label: Let gravity bend the knee
    detail: Let the heel slide slowly down the wall, allowing the knee to bend, using gravity rather
      than muscle.
  - label: Rest at your limit
    detail: Let it slide to a firm stretch and rest there.
  - label: Push back up
    detail: Use your hands or the other foot to help push the heel back up the wall to the start.
dosage:
  reps: 10 to 20 repetitions
  sets: 1 to 3 sets per session
  frequency: 2 to 3 times a day
  hold: 5 seconds at the end of range
  note: Commonly described as 10 to 15 slides, or held at the end of the range for 30 to 60
    seconds, once or twice a day.

  - Recent knee surgery where your surgeon has set a range-of-motion limit
  - Acute swelling with heat or redness

  - Sharp pain rather than a stretch
  - The knee catches part-way down
  - Increased swelling afterwards

  - wall

A heel slide turned through ninety degrees so gravity works for you instead of against you.

With the leg vertical, the weight of the lower leg gently bends the knee rather than
resisting the bend. It is a small change and it makes the movement considerably easier to
relax into — which matters, because relaxing is the mechanism. A knee bent by a guarding
hamstring is not being stretched, it is being pulled against.

Useful when the flat heel slide is hard to do without gripping.
