Losing the last part of straightening matters far more than the number of degrees suggests.

A fully straight knee is mechanically locked — the joint surfaces rotate into a close-packed
position and the skeleton holds you up. A knee that stops a few degrees short never reaches
that position, so the quadriceps works during every second of standing. The knee feels tired
as well as stiff, and the tiredness is real rather than imagined.

Recovering it needs two things together: length at the back of the joint, and a quadriceps
strong enough to hold the very end of the range once it is available.
