A full prostration is, mechanically, a controlled descent to the floor and a controlled return —
performed repeatedly, often on a hard surface, and often many times in a session.

Three demands stack. The knee has to fold to its deepest range while carrying weight, which is
more than almost any daily activity asks. The quadriceps has to lengthen under load on the way
down, which is the harder direction. And the tops of the feet come to the floor, so the ankle
needs to point freely or the knee absorbs the difference.

The repetition matters as much as the depth. A knee that manages one comfortably may not manage
twenty, and that is an endurance problem rather than a range problem — which changes what is
worth working on.

Two practical notes that are not about exercise at all. A folded mat or cloth under the knees
removes the hard-floor contact that causes much of the trouble, and it changes nothing about the
practice. And lowering via a kneeling position with the hands taking some weight asks
considerably less of the knee than dropping straight down.
