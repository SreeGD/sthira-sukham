Walking asks for surprisingly little range — under 90° of bend, which most stiff knees already
have. What it asks for instead is endurance in the muscles that stabilise, repeated over
thousands of steps.

That changes what is worth working on. Stretching does relatively little here. What helps is
the hip holding the pelvis level on each step, and the calf still pushing off at the end of a
long walk as well as it did at the start.

This is also the goal with the best evidence behind it: exercise for knee osteoarthritis has
been studied extensively, and improved walking function is one of its clearest outcomes.
