The knee stiffens from sitting, but it is rarely where the change happened.

Hours at ninety degrees shorten the hip flexors at one end and the calf at the other. Both
then alter how the knee sits and moves, and the glutes — which do nothing at all while you
are in a chair — are slow to take over again when you stand.

So the movements here are weighted toward the hip and the ankle. If that seems like the wrong
place to work on a knee, the entries for iliopsoas and gluteus medius explain why it is not.
