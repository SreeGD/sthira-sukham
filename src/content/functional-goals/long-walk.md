A parikrama of several hours is an endurance event, and it is worth preparing for as one.

The range demand is modest — walking needs under 90° of knee bend, which most stiff knees already
have. What runs out is not range but the stamina of the muscles that stabilise. Gluteus medius
holds the pelvis level on every single step; when it tires, the pelvis drops, the thigh rolls
inward, and the knee starts taking load at an angle it was managing fine three hours earlier.

Uneven ground raises the demand substantially. Every irregularity is a small correction the hip
and ankle have to make, and thousands of those are far more tiring than the same distance on a
flat path. Walking barefoot adds more again, since the foot and calf do work that a shoe would
otherwise share.

So preparation is mostly build-up and endurance rather than stretching. Progressively longer
walks in the weeks beforehand do more than anything on this page, and the movements listed here
support that rather than replace it.

One thing worth saying plainly: a knee that aches at hour two of training will ache at hour two
on the day. That is information to act on beforehand, not to push through.
