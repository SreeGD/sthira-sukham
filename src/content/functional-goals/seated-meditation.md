Cross-legged sitting is where a great many knees are quietly injured, and almost always for the
same reason.

The position needs the thigh to rotate outward at the hip. When the hip will not rotate that far,
the shin becomes a long lever and the rotation is taken from the knee instead — a joint with very
little rotation to give, especially when bent and loaded. Held for an hour, that is a slow strain
rather than a sharp one, which is why it is often noticed only afterwards.

The single most effective change is height. Sitting up on a firm cushion tips the pelvis forward,
which lets the hips rotate more freely and drops the knees without forcing them. Most people need
considerably more height than they expect, and there is no version of this where a lower seat is
better for the knee.

Supporting the knees is the second change. A cushion under each knee removes the hanging weight
that does the levering. It is not a compromise: it is what lets the position be held for hours
rather than minutes.

Work here belongs almost entirely at the hip. The movements below open hip rotation so the knee
does not have to supply it.
