In osteoarthritis the cartilage covering the joint surfaces thins and the bone underneath
remodels, sometimes forming extra bone at the joint margins. The surfaces are less smooth
and the edges are less clear, and the joint moves less freely as a result.

Two things are worth knowing here, because they are commonly misunderstood.

**The stiffness has a pattern.** Osteoarthritic stiffness is typically worst on waking or
after sitting for a while, and eases within about half an hour of moving. Morning stiffness
that lasts substantially longer than that points somewhere else — which is why clinical
guidance uses that thirty-minute mark as part of how osteoarthritis is distinguished from
inflammatory arthritis.

**Movement is the first-line treatment, not a risk to the joint.** Exercise is recommended
by every current international guideline for knee osteoarthritis, and the evidence for it
reducing pain and improving function is among the strongest in this whole field. The
instinct to protect an arthritic knee by using it less makes the stiffness worse.
