Tendons in the foot run through tight tunnels and around sharp corners, and they depend on being able to glide inside their sheaths. After injury, surgery or a long spell in a boot, those surfaces can adhere.

The plantar fascia heals the same way. Repeated small tears at the heel repair with thickened, disorganised tissue rather than the elastic band that was there before — which is why a fascia that has been irritated for months feels stiff first thing in the morning and eases once it has been moved.

Adhesion is characteristically worst after stillness and better after movement, which is close to the opposite of how an irritated joint behaves.
