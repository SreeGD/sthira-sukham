The knee sits inside a fibrous sleeve — the joint capsule — reinforced at the front by
the retinaculum, sheets of tissue that hold the kneecap in its track. Healthy capsule is
pliable and lets the joint travel its full range.

When the capsule tightens, it stops behaving like a sleeve and starts behaving like a
strap. The restriction has a characteristic feel: firm, springy, and consistent, arriving
at the same point in the range every time rather than varying with the day or with how
warmed up you are.

This is one of the reasons stiffness is often worst after a long period of stillness.
Tissue that has been held in one position for hours has to be gradually reminded of the
rest of the range, which is why the first few minutes of movement in the morning are
frequently the hardest of the day.
