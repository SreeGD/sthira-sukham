The big toe joint is one of the most common sites of arthritic change in the whole body, and the stiffness it causes has a distinctive character: hard and abrupt rather than springy, as bone approaches bone.

A bump on the top of the joint is the visible version of the same process. It is worth knowing that the orthopaedic guidance treats the bump as a late sign — by the time it is obvious, bone spurs have generally already formed. Stiffness in the big toe is therefore worth raising early rather than waiting for something to see.

Arthritic change is not a reason to stop moving the joint. It is a reason to stop forcing it.
