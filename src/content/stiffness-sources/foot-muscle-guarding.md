A painful foot gets protected, and the protection is largely involuntary. The toes claw slightly, the calf stays tense, and the foot is set down more carefully and rolled through less.

The difficulty specific to the foot is that you cannot rest it the way you can rest a knee — walking to the kitchen still loads it. So a guarded foot keeps being used in its guarded pattern, which is what turns a short-lived protective response into a habit that outlasts the reason for it.

Guarding eases with warmth and gentle movement, which is the usual way to tell it apart from a restriction in the joint itself.
