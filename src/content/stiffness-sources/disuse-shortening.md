Soft tissue adapts to what is asked of it. Spend most of the day with the hips and knees
bent at roughly ninety degrees — which is to say, sitting — and the tissues settle into
that as their comfortable operating range. The ends of the range are not lost through
damage; they are simply relinquished through disuse.

This is the most common source of stiffness in people with no injury and no diagnosis, and
the most reversible. It is also the least dramatic, which is why it is often assumed to be
something worse.

Two structures do most of the work here, and neither crosses the knee alone. The hip
flexors shorten at the front of the hip, and the calf shortens at the back of the ankle.
Both then influence how the knee sits and moves, which is why a stiff-feeling knee is so
often a hip or ankle problem wearing a disguise.
