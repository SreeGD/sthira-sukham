This is the foot's most avoidable source of stiffness, and the one modern life produces most reliably.

A stiff, cushioned, slightly heeled shoe does several of the foot's jobs for it. It supports the arch, so the small muscles inside the foot do less. It rocks you through the step, so the big toe bends less far. Spend most of your waking hours in one and the foot adapts to the range it is actually asked for, which is a fraction of the range it has.

Nothing here is an argument against supportive footwear. It is an argument for the foot getting some time each day to hold itself up and to bend the toe fully — because tissue keeps what it uses.
