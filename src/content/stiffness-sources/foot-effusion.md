Fluid inside a small joint has nowhere to go, so a quantity that would barely register at the knee can noticeably restrict a toe.

Swelling across the top of the foot or around the base of the big toe stiffens the joint mechanically and, separately, damps the muscles around it. Both effects resolve as the swelling does, which is why the useful response is to settle the swelling rather than to stretch into the restriction it is causing.
