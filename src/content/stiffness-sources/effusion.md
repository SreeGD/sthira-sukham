The knee normally holds a small amount of lubricating fluid. When the joint is irritated
it can produce considerably more, and because the capsule only stretches so far, that
extra volume has to go somewhere. The joint finds the position where it has the most
internal room — usually slightly bent — and resists leaving it.

This produces a stiffness that feels like fullness or pressure rather than tightness, and
it is one of the few kinds that can change noticeably within a single day.

Fluid also does something less obvious: it interferes with the quadriceps' ability to
switch on properly, which is why a swollen knee often feels both stiff and weak at the
same time. The two are connected, not coincidental.

A knee that is swollen **and hot or red** is a different matter entirely and belongs on
the list of reasons to stop and see someone.
