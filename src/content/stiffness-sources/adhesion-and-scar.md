Movement at the knee depends on layers sliding across one another — capsule over bone,
tendon through sheath, kneecap along its groove. After surgery, injury, or a long period
of immobilisation, healing tissue can bind those layers where they should stay free.

The resulting restriction is typically the most stubborn kind. Unlike guarding it does not
ease with warming up, and unlike effusion it does not vary much day to day. It tends to
sit at a fixed point in the range and stay there.

If you have had knee surgery, this is the source most likely to be relevant to you — and
also the one where a surgeon's or physiotherapist's specific instructions matter most.
Post-surgical range restrictions are set deliberately, and general guidance cannot know
what yours are.
