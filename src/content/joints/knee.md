The knee is two joints sharing one space, and they fail differently.

The **tibiofemoral joint** is the main hinge — thigh bone on shin bone — and it carries your
weight. The **patellofemoral joint** is the kneecap running in a groove on the front of the thigh
bone, acting as a pulley that holds the quadriceps tendon away from the joint so the muscle has
better leverage.

A fully straight knee is not simply at the end of its range: it is mechanically locked. The joint
surfaces rotate slightly into a close-packed position that lets you stand without holding yourself
up with muscle. A small muscle behind the knee unlocks it before bending can begin.

Everything below the knee and above it changes what it has to do — which is why this reference
spends as much time on the hip and the ankle as on the joint itself.
