The hip is a ball and socket, and that single structural fact explains most of how it differs from
the knee.

A hinge moves in essentially one plane. A ball and socket moves in all of them — flexion and
extension, out to the side and back, and rotation in both directions. So where a stiff knee is
usually short of bend or short of straightening, a stiff hip can be short in any of six
directions, and which one matters depends entirely on what you are trying to do.

**Rotation is the direction most often lost and least often noticed.** Nothing in a sedentary day
asks the hip to rotate, so it is quietly given up — and it is the direction that sitting
cross-legged, getting out of a car, and turning while walking all depend on.

Restriction here comes in two broad flavours that feel different. Capsular restriction is firm and
consistent, arriving at the same point regardless of warm-up. Muscular restriction eases as you
move and varies between days. Telling them apart matters because they respond to different things.

The hip's relationship to the knee runs mostly through rotation and through the pelvis. When the
hip does not control the thigh bone under load, the groove the kneecap runs in moves underneath
it — the kneecap has not shifted, but its track has.
