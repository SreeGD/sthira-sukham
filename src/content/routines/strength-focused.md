---
title: Strength-focused set
purpose: >-
  For knees that have their range back and now need capacity — loading the quadriceps and hip
  progressively, without impact.
approxDurationMinutes: 20
equipment: [none, wall, chair, towel, cushion]
order: 30
steps:
  - exercise: terminal-knee-extension
    note: Start with the last few degrees of straightening, which are the first thing lost and the hardest to recover.
  - exercise: pilates-footwork
    note: Loaded, but lying down, with the knee alignment visible throughout.
  - exercise: pilates-bridging
    note: Hip extension with the spine articulating segment by segment.
  - exercise: taichi-slow-flexion-extension
    note: Now standing. The slow descent is the demanding half — do not rush it.
  - exercise: taichi-bow-stance
    note: A split stance, which is closest to what walking actually asks. Keep the back heel down.
  - exercise: pilates-standing-leg-pump
    note: Finish with knee and ankle together, which is most of what makes stairs hard.
orderRationale: >-
  Lying to standing, isolated to integrated. The first three steps load the knee and hip in
  positions where body weight is not a factor and alignment can be seen and corrected. The last
  three add body weight and then a split stance, in the order in which the pattern needs to hold
  up. Loading a movement pattern before it is reliable simply makes the compensation stronger,
  which is why the standing work comes last rather than first.
sources: [cochrane-exercise-knee-oa-2024, neumann-kinesiology-2016]
---

For when the stiffness is largely resolved and the remaining problem is that the leg tires,
gives way on stairs, or does not feel trustworthy.

Everything here is body weight and slow. There is no impact and no jumping, which keeps it
usable for knees that still have good and bad days.
