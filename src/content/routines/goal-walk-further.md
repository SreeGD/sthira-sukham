---
title: Walking further — 30 minutes
purpose: >-
  A half-hour session for endurance rather than range: the hip holding the pelvis level step
  after step, and a calf that keeps pushing off at the end of a walk.
approxDurationMinutes: 30
equipment: [none, wall, chair, resistance-band]
order: 160
goal: walk-further
weeklyPlan: >-
  Pair this with progressively longer walks. The session builds the muscles; the walking builds
  the tolerance, and one does not substitute for the other. Two sessions a week alongside walking
  is a reasonable rhythm.
steps:
  - exercise: pilates-clam
    note: 4 minutes. Isolate the side of the hip before loading it.
  - exercise: pilates-side-lying-series
    note: 4 minutes. Same muscle, longer lever.
  - exercise: band-lateral-walk
    note: 4 minutes. Standing and loaded.
  - exercise: pilates-standing-leg-pump
    note: 5 minutes. Knee and calf together, which is most of push-off.
  - exercise: taichi-weight-shifting
    note: 4 minutes. Slow transfers, no momentum to borrow.
  - exercise: taichi-heel-toe-walking
    note: 5 minutes. Walking taken apart and done deliberately.
  - exercise: taichi-standing-post
    note: 4 minutes. Endurance in a bent-knee position, standing still.
orderRationale: >-
  Isolated to integrated, lying to standing. The clam and side-lying work switch the hip on where
  it cannot be substituted for; the banded and standing work then ask for the same thing under
  load; the walking drills apply it to the actual movement. Standing post closes because it is
  pure endurance, and endurance is what this goal runs out of.
sources: [rowe-knee-functional-rom-2000, cochrane-exercise-knee-oa-2024, nascimento-hip-knee-pfp-2018]
---

Walking needs less range than almost anything else and more stamina than most people expect.

Stretching features barely at all here, which is deliberate. What fails on a long walk is not
length — it is the hip tiring and letting the pelvis drop.
