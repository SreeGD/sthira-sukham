---
title: Desk-worker reset
purpose: >-
  For knees stiffened by sitting rather than by injury — addressing the hip and ankle
  restrictions that a day in a chair produces, which is where this kind of stiffness
  actually originates.
approxDurationMinutes: 12
equipment: [none, wall, chair, cushion, towel]
order: 20
steps:
  - exercise: anjaneyasana
    note: The direct answer to hours of hip flexion. Pad the back knee properly.
  - exercise: bent-knee-calf-stretch
    note: The bent-knee version reaches soleus, which governs the positions sitting takes away.
  - exercise: pilates-side-lying-series
    note: Gluteus medius spends the working day switched off. This turns it back on.
  - exercise: setu-bandha
    note: Hip extension under a little load, asking the glutes rather than the hamstrings to do it.
  - exercise: taichi-knee-over-toe
    note: Finish by checking the pattern under load. A mirror helps more than concentration here.
orderRationale: >-
  Length before strength, hip before knee. The first two steps restore what sitting has
  shortened — hip flexors and calf — because asking a muscle to work through a range it no
  longer has produces compensation rather than strength. The last three then train the hip
  muscles that sitting leaves dormant. The knee itself is barely addressed directly, which is
  the point: in this pattern the knee is reporting a problem it did not create.
sources: [neumann-kinesiology-2016, cochrane-exercise-knee-oa-2024]
---

Aimed at the pattern where there is no injury and no diagnosis — the knee simply feels tight,
worse after a long day at a desk, and worst on standing up after a meeting.

It is deliberately weighted toward the hip and ankle. If that seems like the wrong place to
work on a knee, the muscle pages for iliopsoas and gluteus medius explain why it is not.
