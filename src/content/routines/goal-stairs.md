---
title: Stairs — 30 minutes
purpose: >-
  A half-hour session for going downstairs with more confidence: the bend to clear each step,
  and the controlled lowering that going down actually demands.
approxDurationMinutes: 30
equipment: [none, wall, chair, towel, resistance-band]
order: 110
goal: stairs
weeklyPlan: >-
  Two or three sessions a week works better than one. If a single weekly session is what you
  have, keep it — but add the two short strength steps on one other day, which takes five
  minutes and does more than lengthening this session would.
steps:
  - exercise: heel-slide
    note: 4 minutes. Open the bending range before asking it to carry weight.
  - exercise: quad-sets
    note: 3 minutes. Wake the thigh up before loading it.
  - exercise: terminal-knee-extension
    note: 4 minutes. The last part of straightening, which stairs need on every step.
  - exercise: pilates-clam
    note: 4 minutes. The hip muscle that stops the knee falling inward as you descend.
  - exercise: band-lateral-walk
    note: 4 minutes. The same muscle, now standing and under load.
  - exercise: taichi-slow-flexion-extension
    note: 5 minutes. Slow controlled lowering, without a step to fall off.
  - exercise: step-down
    note: 6 minutes. The movement itself. Start on a single book, not a stair.
orderRationale: >-
  Range, then activation, then hip, then the movement. Opening the bend first means the
  loaded work happens in a range the knee actually has. The hip work sits before the step-down
  deliberately: the knee drifting inward is a hip failure, and asking for the pattern before
  the muscle is switched on trains the fault rather than the fix. The step-down comes last
  because it is the only step here you can do badly without noticing until afterwards.
sources: [rowe-knee-functional-rom-2000, nascimento-hip-knee-pfp-2018, cochrane-exercise-knee-oa-2024]
---

Built around one specific complaint: going down is harder than going up.

Most of this session is preparation for the last six minutes. That is deliberate — a step-down
done with a knee that will not fully straighten, or a hip that will not hold the pelvis level,
rehearses the exact pattern that makes stairs uncomfortable.
