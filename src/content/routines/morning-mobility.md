---
title: Ten-minute morning mobility
purpose: >-
  A short sequence for knees that are stiffest on waking — working from fully supported
  positions toward standing, roughly in the order the body tends to tolerate first thing.
approxDurationMinutes: 10
equipment: [none, wall, strap, towel]
order: 10
steps:
  - exercise: heel-slide
    note: Start here. Lying down, nothing to balance, and the joint moves without the muscles having to work.
  - exercise: prone-hang
    note: Straightening before standing. Give it a few minutes rather than rushing it.
  - exercise: supta-padangusthasana
    note: Still lying down. The strap does the holding so the back of the leg can relax.
  - exercise: straight-leg-calf-stretch
    note: First standing movement. Hands on the wall, so balance is not yet a demand.
  - exercise: taichi-weight-shifting
    note: Finish upright and loaded, but slowly. This bridges the gap between the floor and the rest of your day.
orderRationale: >-
  Supported to unsupported, and passive to active. Morning stiffness is at its worst before
  anything has moved, so the sequence starts with positions that ask nothing of the muscles —
  lying down, gravity doing the work — and only reaches standing and weight-bearing once the
  joint has been through its range a few times. The reverse order is what most people
  instinctively do, and it is why the first attempt at stairs so often goes badly.
sources: [cochrane-exercise-knee-oa-2024, aaos-knee-arthritis]
---

Built for the half hour after waking, when osteoarthritic stiffness in particular is typically
at its worst and typically eases with movement.

Nothing here is strenuous, and that is intentional. This is about visiting the range, not
training. If you only have five minutes, the first two steps carry most of the value.
