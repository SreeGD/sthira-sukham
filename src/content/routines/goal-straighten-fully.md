---
title: Getting it straight — 30 minutes
purpose: >-
  A half-hour session for the last few degrees of extension: length behind the knee, and a
  quadriceps that can hold the joint once the range is available.
approxDurationMinutes: 30
equipment: [none, wall, towel, strap, chair, resistance-band]
order: 150
goal: straighten-fully
weeklyPlan: >-
  The prone hang is worth doing on more days than this session. Five to ten minutes of it, most
  days, does more for extension than any weekly session — it is time under a very small load
  that changes passive tissue.
steps:
  - exercise: prone-hang
    note: 8 minutes. The longest step here, and the most important. Do nothing.
  - exercise: supta-padangusthasana
    note: 5 minutes. Hamstring length, pelvis held down by the floor.
  - exercise: quad-sets
    note: 4 minutes. Reactivate the muscle that holds the end range.
  - exercise: terminal-knee-extension
    note: 5 minutes. The last fifteen degrees, isolated.
  - exercise: band-terminal-knee-extension
    note: 5 minutes. The same range, now with resistance that grows as you straighten.
  - exercise: tadasana
    note: 3 minutes. Standing without pushing the knee backward past straight.
orderRationale: >-
  Passive length first, then active holding. The prone hang and the hamstring stretch make range
  available; the quadriceps work teaches the knee to occupy it. Doing the strength work first
  would train the muscle to hold a range the joint does not yet have. Mountain pose closes
  because the opposite fault — locking the knee backward past straight — is the habit people
  fall into once extension returns.
sources: [rowe-knee-functional-rom-2000, neumann-kinesiology-2016, aaos-knee-arthritis]
---

Losing the last few degrees of straightening costs more than the number suggests, because a knee
that never locks is a knee whose quadriceps works all day.

This session is deliberately front-loaded with one long, dull, passive step. That is where the
change comes from.
