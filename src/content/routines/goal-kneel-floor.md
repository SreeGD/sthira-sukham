---
title: Kneeling and floor transfers — 30 minutes
purpose: >-
  A half-hour session for getting down to the floor and back: deep knee bend, length through the
  front of the thigh, and enough ankle movement to keep the position tolerable.
approxDurationMinutes: 30
equipment: [none, wall, cushion, towel, strap, chair]
order: 120
goal: kneel-floor
weeklyPlan: >-
  Range at the far end of a joint responds to being visited often rather than forced once. A
  weekly 30-minute session plus five minutes of heel slides on two or three other days will do
  considerably more than the session alone.
steps:
  - exercise: heel-slide
    note: 5 minutes. The gentlest way into deep bending, with nothing to carry.
  - exercise: heel-slide-with-strap
    note: 4 minutes. A little assistance at the end of the range.
  - exercise: prone-quad-stretch
    note: 4 minutes. Length at the front of the thigh, with the hip straight.
  - exercise: bent-knee-calf-stretch
    note: 4 minutes. Bent-knee ankle range, which kneeling and squatting both need.
  - exercise: balasana
    note: 5 minutes. Deep bend with weight, padded. Towel behind the knees.
  - exercise: virasana
    note: 4 minutes. Only if child's pose is comfortable. Sit high.
  - exercise: malasana
    note: 4 minutes. Sit onto a cushion at whatever height you have.
orderRationale: >-
  Unloaded before loaded, and short-lever before long. The heel slides open the range with the
  leg supported; the stretches lengthen the two structures that most often limit it; only then
  does body weight arrive. Child's pose comes before hero pose because it is the same demand
  with an escape route — you can shift your weight forward onto your hands at any point, which
  hero pose does not allow.
sources: [rowe-knee-functional-rom-2000, neumann-kinesiology-2016, yoga-knee-oa-meta-2024]
---

The most range-hungry goal in this reference, and the one where patience matters most.

Nothing here should be forced. A knee that is pushed into deep flexion under weight before it
has the range unloaded tends to respond by guarding, which costs you the range you were trying
to gain.
