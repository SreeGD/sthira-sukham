The most recognisable foot presentation there is, and it has a signature that separates it from
almost everything else in this reference: **sharp pain on the first few steps after rest, easing
as you keep moving.**

That pattern — worst after stillness, better with movement, worse again by the end of a long
day — is the thing to notice. Most joint restrictions do not behave that way.

Several factors stack. A calf that is short keeps the heel under tension. The small muscles inside
the foot, which should hold the arch actively, contribute less than they could, leaving the fascia
carrying the arch alone. And the fascia is passive tissue that does not adapt to overload the way
muscle does; it simply gets irritated.

The reason it belongs in a stiffness reference rather than a pain one is that the calf and the
arch are usually both stiff, and the stiffness is upstream of the pain rather than a consequence
of it.

The clinical guideline for this presentation is unusually clear that a combination of approaches
outperforms any single one — stretching alone is the common mistake.
