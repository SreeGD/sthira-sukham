Pain and tenderness over the bony point on the side of the hip, typically worse lying on that
side, on stairs, and after standing on one leg for a while.

The mechanism usually described involves compression: when the pelvis drops on the unsupported
side, the tissue over that bony point is squeezed against it. So positions that let the leg cross
the midline — sitting cross-legged, standing with weight hanging on one hip, lying with the top
knee dropped forward — tend to aggravate it, and those are habits rather than exercises.

Its relevance to the chain is direct. The muscles involved are the same ones that control whether
the thigh bone rotates inward under load, which is what the knee below is sitting on top of.
