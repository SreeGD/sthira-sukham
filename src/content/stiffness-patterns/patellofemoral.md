This pattern centres on the kneecap and the groove it travels in rather than on the main
hinge of the knee. It is commonly described as stiffness and discomfort at the front of the
knee, more noticeable going down stairs than up, and after sitting for a long stretch with
the knee bent.

The kneecap is held in its track by soft tissue pulling from several directions at once —
the quadriceps above, the retinaculum at the sides, the iliotibial band laterally. An
imbalance between those pulls changes how the kneecap sits, and small changes in tracking
can produce disproportionate stiffness.

The part people find surprising is how often the hip is involved. If the thigh bone rotates
inward because the hip muscles are not controlling it, the groove moves underneath the
kneecap even when nothing about the kneecap itself has changed.

This is a general description of a commonly discussed presentation, not a checklist.
