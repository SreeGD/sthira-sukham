The pattern in people with no injury and no diagnosis: hips that feel tight rather than painful,
worst on standing up after a long sitting spell.

Sitting holds the hip flexed and asks for no rotation at all, so both are quietly given up. The
glutes do nothing for hours and are slow to take over again on standing. The result is a hip that
works fine within the small range it uses and complains at the edges of anything larger.

Of the hip patterns described here, this is the most responsive to simply moving through a fuller
range regularly — particularly in rotation, which almost nothing else in a sedentary day supplies.
