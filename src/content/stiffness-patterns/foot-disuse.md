This is the quietest pattern here, because nothing hurts.

A foot that spends its waking life in a supportive shoe is a foot doing less than it can. The shoe
holds the arch, so the small muscles inside the foot are not asked to. The sole rocks you through
the step, so the big toe bends less far than it could. Tissue keeps what it uses, and over years
the foot narrows its own range to the one it is given.

There is nothing to feel, which is precisely the difficulty. It is usually discovered indirectly:
by a foot that cramps the first time it is asked to grip, toes that claw during balance work, or a
kneeling position that turns out to be limited at the big toe.

The remedy is unglamorous and cheap. Time spent barefoot on safe ground, deliberate work at the
end of the big toe's range, and asking the arch to hold itself up rather than be held.

Nothing here argues against supportive footwear. The argument is only that a foot which never does
its own job eventually cannot.
