The most common ankle pattern, and one with **no knee analogue**: stiffness and a sense of
unreliability persisting long after a sprained ligament has healed.

Several things stack. Swelling occupies space the joint needs. The calf guards protectively. And
because walking cannot be avoided, the ankle keeps being used in a guarded way, which reinforces
the pattern rather than resolving it.

What is characteristically lost is dorsiflexion — the shin travelling forward over the foot — and
that loss is easy to miss because walking on the flat barely needs any. It shows up later, on
stairs, on slopes, and in squatting, often long after the ankle was declared better.

The knee above frequently inherits the problem. When the shin cannot travel forward, the movement
gets taken from elsewhere.
