The picture usually described here is stiffness that is worst first thing in the morning
and after sitting, easing within about half an hour of moving, alongside activity-related
pain. Clinical guidance treats that half-hour mark as meaningful: morning stiffness that
lasts substantially longer is a reason to look at other explanations.

Guidelines describe diagnosing osteoarthritis clinically in people over 45 with
activity-related joint pain and either no morning stiffness or morning stiffness lasting no
more than thirty minutes, without needing imaging to confirm it.

What is worth knowing generally is that exercise is the first-line management in every
current international guideline, with substantial trial evidence behind it, and that the
common instinct — protect the joint by using it less — reliably makes both stiffness and
function worse.

None of which tells you whether this describes your knee. That is a clinical judgement,
and it is a straightforward one for a clinician to make.
