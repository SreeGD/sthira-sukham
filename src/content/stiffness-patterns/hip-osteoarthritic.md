Commonly described as groin pain with activity, stiffness after sitting, and a characteristic
order in which range is lost — rotation first, then extension, then flexion.

Two features catch people out. The pain is often felt in the groin rather than over the hip, and
it can be referred to the knee — so a knee complaint occasionally turns out to belong to the hip
above it. And because rotation goes first and nothing in a sedentary day asks for rotation, the
loss can be well advanced before anything feels wrong.

Guidance for hip osteoarthritis is the same as for the knee: exercise is first-line management,
with the same thirty-minute morning-stiffness distinction used in diagnosis.

This describes a presentation, not your hip. Which pattern applies to a particular person is a
clinical judgement.
