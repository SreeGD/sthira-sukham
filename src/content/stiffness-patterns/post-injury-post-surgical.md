After a significant injury or an operation, several sources of stiffness usually arrive
together: swelling occupying space in the joint, healing tissue binding surfaces that
should glide, and protective muscle guarding — often persisting after the reason for it has
resolved. The quadriceps is characteristically slow to switch back on.

Restoring range is normally an explicit goal of rehabilitation, which is why functional
thresholds get quoted: research measuring knee motion across everyday activities found gait
and slopes need under 90° of flexion, stairs and chairs 90–120°, and getting into a bath
around 135°, with roughly 110° suggested as a sensible rehabilitation target.

This is the pattern where general information is least appropriate as a guide to action.
Post-surgical range restrictions are set deliberately by the surgeon, they differ by
procedure, and no general resource can know yours. Where instructions you have been given
conflict with anything here, follow the instructions.
