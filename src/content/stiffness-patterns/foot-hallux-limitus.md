The foot's signature pattern, and one with **no analogue anywhere else in this reference**: a
single small joint losing a motion that every step depends on.

Walking asks the big toe to bend upward roughly 65° as the heel lifts. When it will not, two
things follow. The windlass mechanism does not wind, so the arch never locks and the foot pushes
off a softer lever than it should. And the body finds the missing range elsewhere — most often by
rolling off the inner edge of the foot instead of over the toe.

That substitution is what carries the problem upward. Rolling off the inside turns the foot in,
turns the shin in with it, and changes the angle the knee is loaded at, on every step.

It usually announces itself gradually: first as stiffness, later as pain at the joint during
walking, later still as a bump on the top of the foot that makes shoes uncomfortable. The bump is
a late sign rather than an early one, which is the argument for raising a stiff big toe early.

Kneeling with the toes tucked under is where most people first notice it, because that position
asks for close to the full range under load.
